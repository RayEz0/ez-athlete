      GYM

      Ten full-body sessions, A–J. Each has three parts: Gym, Athletic, and Core & Cooldown. Tick what you actually completed, then log it. Each session can be logged once per week, and eligibility resets every Monday. Your history is never deleted.

      30-minute emergency home workouts

      Counts as a gym session

      Use one instead of skipping when you can’t get to the gym. No equipment needed, though a loaded backpack works for rows and RDLs.

      15 rotating options

      Plyometrics

      Pick the routine that fits today’s basketball load, gym fatigue and how your shins and knees feel. Keep high-intent days limited; on low days, focus on elasticity and landings. Each routine can be logged once per week.

      Every morning

      Core

      After waking and stretching, do one 5–10 minute routine and rotate through the options. Keep 1–3 clean reps in reserve: the goal is a strong athletic trunk without stealing recovery from basketball or lifting. Each routine can be logged once per week.

      Recovery + prehab

      Protect performance

      Neck / upper-body

      • Neck flexion isometric — 2 × 15–20 sec, gentle and pain-free
      • Neck lateral isometric — 2 × 15–20 sec/side
      • Chin tuck — 2 × 6 with 5-sec holds
      • T-spine extension — 2 × 6, slow

      Ankle / lower leg

      • Ankle dorsiflexion rocks — 2 × 10/side
      • Soleus isometric — 2 × 30–45 sec
      • Tib raises — 2 × 15
      • Hip flexor stretch — 2 × 30 sec/side

      Meditation

      Simple daily option: 5–10 minutes seated, slow nasal breathing, attention on the breath; use it after training or before bed.

      Massage gun

      Use as a recovery tool, not as a substitute for sleep. Keep pressure comfortable; avoid the front/side of the neck and bony areas.

      Sleep

      Target 8 hours; 7 hours is the minimum floor. Your recurring neck symptoms should not be aggressively loaded; persistent/worsening symptoms or new neurological symptoms should be professionally assessed.
