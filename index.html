30-minute emergency home workouts

Use one instead of skipping when you cannot reach the gym. No equipment is required, but a backpack can be loaded for rows/RDLs.

Emergency Home 1

3 rounds: 10 push-ups · 12 split squats/leg · 12 backpack rows · 15 glute bridges · 30–45s plank. Finish with 3×3 controlled squat jumps.

Emergency Home 2

4 rounds: 8 pike push-ups · 10 reverse lunges/leg · 12 backpack RDLs · 10 backpack rows · 20 mountain climbers. Finish with 4×5 pogo contacts.

Emergency Home 3

3 rounds: 12 tempo split squats/leg · 10 push-ups · 12 backpack rows · 10 single-leg RDL/leg · 30s side plank/side. Finish with 5 min mobility.

Craving protocol

  1. Delay 10 min with water and leave the ordering app.
  2. Use a planned substitute: fruit + curd, whey + fruit, roasted chana, popcorn or protein oats.
  3. Walk 10 min easy.
  4. Return to the normal plan at the next meal.
Recovery + prehab

Protect performance

Neck / upper-body

  • Neck flexion isometric — 2 × 15–20 sec, gentle and pain-free
  • Neck lateral isometric — 2 × 15–20 sec/side
  • Chin tuck — 2 × 6 with 5-sec holds
  • T-spine extension — 2 × 6, slow

Ankle / lower leg

  • Ankle dorsiflexion rocks — 2 × 10/side
  • Soleus isometric — 2 × 30–45 sec
  • Tib raises — 2 × 15
  • Hip flexor stretch — 2 × 30 sec/side

Meditation

Simple daily option: 5–10 minutes seated, slow nasal breathing, attention on the breath; use it after training or before bed.

Massage gun

Use as a recovery tool, not as a substitute for sleep. Keep pressure comfortable; avoid the front/side of the neck and bony areas.

Sleep

Target 8 hours; 7 hours is the minimum floor. Your recurring neck symptoms should not be aggressively loaded; persistent/worsening symptoms or new neurological symptoms should be professionally assessed.
